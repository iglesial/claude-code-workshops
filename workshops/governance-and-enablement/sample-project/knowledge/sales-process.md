# Acme Corp — Sales Process & Account Management Guide

**Audience:** Account Executives, Account Managers, Sales Development Representatives  
**Last updated:** February 2025

---

## Company Overview

Acme Corp sells contract lifecycle management (CLM) software. Our platform helps legal and procurement teams draft, approve, sign, and track contracts faster — reducing average contract cycle time from 3–4 weeks to under 5 days.

We compete primarily with DocuSign CLM, Ironclad, and Juro. Our differentiation is:
- Best-in-class template editor (non-lawyers can draft without training)
- Native French and German language support (critical for EU customers)
- Flat-rate pricing with no per-signature fees

We do not win on price. We win on ease of adoption and EU data residency.

---

## Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

We close best with companies that match **all three of these criteria**:

1. **200–2,000 employees** — smaller companies don't have enough contract volume; larger ones have procurement processes that slow us down beyond our capacity
2. **Legal team of 5+ people** — smaller legal teams have no bandwidth to lead a CLM rollout
3. **Currently using Word + email + DocuSign** — this is our primary displacement target; companies already on a CLM platform are rarely worth pursuing

**Strongest verticals:** Retail (supply chain contracts), Banking (vendor agreements), Manufacturing (procurement), Professional services (client MSAs)

**Weakest fit:** Public sector (procurement rules), Startups under 100 employees (no legal team), Companies already on SAP Ariba (too entrenched)

## Deal Stages

### Stage 1 — Discovery (target: 2 weeks)

Goal: Confirm ICP fit and quantify the pain.

Key questions to answer before moving forward:
- What is their current contract cycle time? (Benchmark: >2 weeks = strong pain)
- How many contracts per month does legal handle? (Benchmark: >50 = strong volume)
- Who owns the contract process — legal, procurement, or ops?
- Is there a budget owner and has budget been identified?
- What is the trigger for this conversation — a specific incident, a legal hire, a board ask?

Exit criteria: Pain is confirmed and quantified, budget owner is identified, and there is a named internal champion.

### Stage 2 — Demo (target: 1 week to schedule, 1 session)

Goal: Show the product solving their specific pain, not a generic walkthrough.

Always customise the demo to the top two pain points identified in Discovery. If their pain is slow approvals, lead with the approval workflow. If it's template chaos, lead with the template library.

Never demo features the prospect didn't ask about. We lose deals when we overwhelm legal teams with capabilities they don't need.

### Stage 3 — Proof of Concept (30 days, 3–5 users)

Goal: Prove adoption in the prospect's environment.

POC rules:
- Scope is fixed: template migration (max 5 templates), one approval workflow, e-signature for 10 contracts
- POC is free; we do not discount full contracts to compensate for a paid POC
- A dedicated CSM is assigned from day one of POC
- Success criteria must be agreed in writing before the POC starts

Exit criteria: At least 2 of the 3–5 POC users rate the product "would use daily" in the exit survey.

### Stage 4 — Negotiation

Goal: Close on terms we can deliver, at a price that reflects our value.

Pricing authority:
- AEs can discount up to 10% without approval
- 11–20% requires VP of Sales approval (Slack #sales-approvals, 24h SLA)
- >20% requires CEO sign-off; rare and requires exceptional justification

Never negotiate on data residency or security terms. If a prospect requires data to leave the EU, that is not a deal we can do.

### Stage 5 — Closed Won / Closed Lost

Won: Countersigned contract uploaded to Salesforce within 24 hours. Handoff call with CSM within 3 business days.

Lost: Loss reason logged in Salesforce (mandatory). If the reason is "chose competitor," name the competitor. If the reason is "no decision," document the blocker.

## Account Management Responsibilities (Post-Sale)

Account Managers own customer relationships from the moment the contract is countersigned.

**First 90 days:**
- Kickoff call within 5 business days of signature
- Check-in call at day 30 and day 60
- Confirm go-live date and track against it

**Ongoing:**
- Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs) for all customers >€40k ARR
- Renewal conversations start 120 days before contract end
- Expansion opportunities: flag to AE when customer reaches 80% of their contracted volume

**Churn signals to watch:**
- No logins in 14 days from key users
- Legal team headcount drops (check LinkedIn)
- Parent company acquisition announced
- Customer misses two consecutive QBRs without rescheduling

## Communication Tone Guide

Acme Corp's tone is **consultative, direct, and human**. We are not a startup trying to sound like a tech company. We are not a legacy vendor trying to sound authoritative. We are a trusted partner who happens to build software.

**Do:**
- Lead with the client's pain, not our features ("You mentioned approval bottlenecks cost your team 3 days per contract — here's how we address that")
- Use plain language ("you can see who approved what" not "the platform surfaces granular audit trail data")
- Be direct about limitations ("We don't support SAP integration natively — here's our workaround")

**Don't:**
- Use jargon: "leverage," "synergies," "end-to-end solution," "best-in-class" (except where accurate)
- Promise what you're not sure about — escalate to product instead
- CC too many people on emails — legal teams dislike cc chains

**Email signature standard:**
```
[First Name] [Last Name]
Account Manager, Acme Corp
[mobile] | acmecorp.io
```

No taglines. No quotes. No images except the logo in HTML emails.
